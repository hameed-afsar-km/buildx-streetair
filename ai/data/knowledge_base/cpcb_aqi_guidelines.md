# Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) — National Air Quality Index Guidelines
*Published by Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change, Government of India*

## 1. Overview and Purpose
The National Air Quality Index (AQI) was launched in India in October 2014 under the Swachh Bharat Abhiyan to provide a simple, unified, numerical metric representing air quality. It converts complex air quality measurements of various pollutants into a single number, nomenclature, and color code.

## 2. Pollutants Considered
The CPCB NAQI framework monitors eight critical ambient air pollutants:
1. Particulate Matter 10 (PM10) — 24-hourly average
2. Particulate Matter 2.5 (PM2.5) — 24-hourly average
3. Nitrogen Dioxide (NO2) — 24-hourly average
4. Sulphur Dioxide (SO2) — 24-hourly average
5. Carbon Monoxide (CO) — 8-hourly average
6. Ozone (O3) — 8-hourly average
7. Ammonia (NH3) — 24-hourly average
8. Lead (Pb) — 24-hourly average

For an overall AQI to be computed, a minimum of three pollutants must be monitored, of which at least one must be either PM10 or PM2.5.

## 3. AQI Categories and Health Impact Breakpoints

### Good (0 - 50)
- PM2.5 range: 0 - 30 µg/m³
- PM10 range: 0 - 50 µg/m³
- Health impact: Minimal impact on human health.

### Satisfactory (51 - 100)
- PM2.5 range: 31 - 60 µg/m³
- PM10 range: 51 - 100 µg/m³
- Health impact: Minor breathing discomfort to sensitive people.

### Moderate (101 - 200)
- PM2.5 range: 61 - 90 µg/m³
- PM10 range: 101 - 250 µg/m³
- Health impact: Breathing discomfort to people with lungs, asthma, and heart diseases.

### Poor (201 - 300)
- PM2.5 range: 91 - 120 µg/m³
- PM10 range: 251 - 350 µg/m³
- Health impact: Breathing discomfort to most people on prolonged exposure.

### Very Poor (301 - 400)
- PM2.5 range: 121 - 250 µg/m³
- PM10 range: 351 - 430 µg/m³
- Health impact: Respiratory illness on prolonged exposure.

### Severe (401 - 500)
- PM2.5 range: 250+ µg/m³
- PM10 range: 430+ µg/m³
- Health impact: Affects healthy people and seriously impacts those with existing diseases.

## 4. Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP) for NCR & Northern India
- Stage I (Poor: AQI 201-300): Water sprinkling on roads, strict mechanized sweeping, ban on open burning.
- Stage II (Very Poor: AQI 301-400): Diesel generator set bans except essential services, increased parking fees to discourage private vehicles, enhanced bus and metro frequencies.
- Stage III (Severe: AQI 401-450): Strict ban on non-essential construction and demolition activities, restrictions on BS-III petrol and BS-IV diesel light motor vehicles.
- Stage IV (Severe+: AQI >450): Four-wheeler diesel vehicle restrictions, possible closure of educational institutions, work from home for public/private offices.
